1"""Composition-root bridge: drive the Supply status observer from BLEPS PVs. 

2 

3The Supply BC's `SupplyObserver` port is BC-local (`cora.supply.ports`) 

4and the `ControlPort` value-IO it needs is Operation-BC-owned 

5(`cora.operation.ports`). tach forbids `cora.supply -> cora.operation`, 

6so the bridging adapter lives here at the composition root, exactly as 

7`_enclosure_permit_observer` does. If a third cross-BC `ControlPort` 

8consumer appears, the rule-of-three move is to hoist `ControlPort` to 

9`cora.infrastructure.ports` and relocate both adapters into their BCs. 

10 

11## Many channels, one Supply 

12 

13Unlike the enclosure permit observer (one PV per Enclosure), a Supply 

14here is fed by many channels: eight cooling-water circuits behind 

15`2-BM cooling water`, and seven vacuum sections plus four valves behind 

16`2-BM beamline vacuum`. The Supply's status is the aggregate, and the 

17channel that caused it travels in the reason and in `monitor_ref`. 

18 

19## Two axes, not three severities 

20 

21BLEPS sorts channels into warnings, trips and faults, and those are not 

22one scale (see `docs/deployments/2-bm/operations.md`). This adapter 

23implements the two axes: 

24 

25 - **process** (`trip`): the measured value crossed its limit, or a 

26 valve disobeyed. Drives status. 

27 - **trust** (`fault`): the reading is not believable. An off-scale 

28 reading pointing away from danger is physically implausible, so 

29 BLEPS reports it as a broken instrument. Drives nothing; it removes 

30 that channel from the verdict. 

31 

32`Communications_Fault` is the trust axis for the whole system: while it 

33stands, or while its own PV cannot be believably read, no observation is 

34emitted at all. 

35 

36Warnings are wired, gated off by default. See "Warnings, gated off by 

37default" below. 

38 

39## Clear requires ALL channels, tripped requires only one 

40 

41The asymmetry is the safety property, and getting it wrong was a real 

42fail-open bug caught in gate review. A Supply is reported: 

43 

44 - **tripped** when ANY believable channel trips, and 

45 - **clear** only when EVERY channel is believable AND clear. 

46 

47Anything else withholds. The tempting version, "no trips among the 

48channels I trust", is wrong: if the one channel carrying a standing trip 

49loses its instrument (or its subscription, or its quality flag) while a 

50sibling still reads clear, that version concludes the trip cleared. It 

51did not. It became unobservable, and those are opposite facts. So losing 

52sight of any channel costs the Supply its ability to be called clear, 

53and a resource stays down until it can be seen to be up. 

54 

55## Stateless: levels, not edges 

56 

57The adapter holds no per-Supply memory. It recomputes the verdict on 

58every reading and emits it, and the runtime decides what it means 

59against the Supply's real status. An earlier version tracked edges here, 

60which produced three separate defects: a lost append was never re-sent 

61(the enclosure precedent is immune precisely because it emits levels), a 

62re-subscribe reset the memory and stranded a Supply at `Unavailable`, 

63and the memory disagreed with the aggregate whenever an operator moved 

64it by hand. The aggregate's own status is the only trustworthy record of 

65where a Supply is, and the runtime is where it is readable. 

66 

67Emission is naturally sparse despite being level-based, because EPICS 

68monitors fire on change: a beamline with nothing happening produces no 

69readings after the initial connect. 

70 

71`Available` is never emitted. The furthest a monitor goes is 

72`Recovering`, and `cora.supply._monitor` drops even that unless the 

73Supply is actually `Unavailable`; a person closes the loop with 

74`restore_supply`. 

75 

76## Probes: an inconclusive verdict is a reach fact, not silence 

77 

78The withhold case (a believable trip cannot be distinguished from an 

79unbelievable one, or the whole comms feed is dark) used to emit nothing 

80at all for the affected Supply. That is exactly the ambiguity the 

81probe trail exists to resolve: a Supply CORA cannot currently assess 

82must not look, from the trail's perspective, identical to one nobody 

83is watching. `_observations` now reports every configured Supply on 

84every call, either a real verdict (`reach_tier=RELAYED`) or a 

85probe-only entry (`observed_status=None`, `reach_tier=UNREACHED`). 

86`cora.supply._monitor` writes the probe row unconditionally and returns 

87before attempting a transition when `observed_status` is `None`, so a 

88withheld verdict never becomes a false Supply status. 

89 

90## Probe ticks: one poller per Supply, reading every channel 

91 

92When `probe_tick_seconds` is configured, each Supply in scope also gets 

93a sibling polling task (`_poll`) alongside the per-PV push subscriptions 

94(`_pump`), both feeding the same queue. EPICS CA monitors are 

95change-only, so a beamline where nothing is happening produces no 

96readings at all after the initial connect, and the trail would then show 

97a gap that reads as a coverage outage when CORA was in fact still 

98watching. That is the exact ambiguity this seam exists to resolve. 

99 

100The tick is scoped to a SUPPLY, not to a PV, because that is what a 

101probe row is keyed by and what a reader of the trail is asking about. 

102It reads every PV behind that Supply (each channel's fault, trip and 

103warning, plus the system-wide comms flag, which gates this Supply's 

104verdict as much as its own channels do) and writes ONE row: `RELAYED` 

105only if every one of them answered, `UNREACHED` naming the first that 

106did not. That all-or-nothing grading is the same asymmetry `_verdict` 

107already applies, for the same reason: losing sight of any one channel 

108costs the Supply its claim to being seen, because a trail that says 

109"watched" while a circuit is dark is worth less than no trail at all. 

110Per-PV rows were the alternative and would multiply two rows a tick into 

111thirty-two without answering a question the aggregate row leaves open. 

112 

113The poller is a SIBLING of the pumps rather than nested inside one, for 

114the reason the enclosure precedent gives: a pump returns as soon as its 

115subscription ends, and `_drain` only re-subscribes once every pump has 

116returned, so a poller living inside a pump would die with it and could 

117never observe a recovery. As a sibling it keeps ticking through a dead 

118push path, and its probes are then the only remaining signal about which 

119specific PVs are unreachable. 

120 

121A tick NEVER carries a status claim and never touches `latest`, so it 

122cannot move a Supply. That is a deliberate line rather than an omission. 

123It keeps exactly one path able to change a Supply's status, the push 

124path that carries the whole believability fold and all of its tests, and 

125it keeps `ReachTier`'s promise honest: a successful poll proves the 

126configured channels answered this tick, never that the verdict standing 

127in `latest` is current. Using poll reads to REFRESH `latest` would be a 

128different feature (repairing a silently dropped CA monitor update) 

129carrying its own unanswered question, what a failed read should do to a 

130value a subscription reported perfectly well a moment earlier, and 

131settling that by accident inside a coverage-trail change is how a 

132fail-open arrives unreviewed. 

133 

134## Warnings, gated off by default 

135 

136The 30 warning channels map to `Degraded`, one severity rung below a 

137trip, following the same one-is-enough-to-degrade / all-must-clear 

138asymmetry `_verdict` already applies to trips. This was blocked at 

139first: `Degraded -> Available` was a one-way door in the shipped Supply 

140FSM (`restore_supply` accepted only `Recovering`), so a latched warning 

141nobody reset would have parked a resource in a state an operator could 

142not leave without first declaring it Unavailable. That gap closed when 

143`restore_supply` widened to accept `{Recovering, Degraded}`. 

144 

145What is still open is not semantic but empirical: nobody has measured 

146how often BLEPS warnings actually latch at 2-BM. A channel that warns 

147routinely would still park its Supply in `Degraded` semi-permanently, 

148which fails the run-start supply gate (`Degraded` does not satisfy it). 

149Rather than block on staff answering that, warnings ship OFF by 

150default, gated by `Settings.bleps_supply_warnings_enabled`: this 

151adapter is unconditionally warning-AWARE (`BlepsChannel.warning_pv`, 

152`_verdict`'s second return element), but the composition root only 

153populates `warning_pv` when the flag is on. A deployment can flip it 

154and observe the real base rate directly instead of guessing at it. 

155 

156A monitor can drive `Degraded` only from `{Unknown, Available, 

157Recovering}` (the decider's `_DEGRADABLE_SOURCES`), so a warning 

158observed while a Supply sits `Unavailable` from an earlier trip cannot 

159degrade it further; `cora.supply._monitor` skips that case before 

160attempting the command, mirroring its existing `Recovering`-vs- 

161`Unavailable` skip. 

162"""

218 wired -- the default, gated by `Settings.bleps_supply_warnings_enabled`

230def flag_state_from_reading(reading: Measurement) -> bool | None: 

231 """Is this BLEPS flag high? `None` when the reading cannot be believed. 

232 

233 Named `<value>_from_reading` rather than `is_*` because the third 

234 answer is load-bearing: a reading this function cannot believe or 

235 cannot interpret is not a LOW reading, and conflating the two would 

236 let a dead PV read as "no fault here". 

237 

238 ## The quality floor is `Bad`, because on an interlock the alarm IS 

239 ## the signal 

240 

241 This asks "can I believe this value", not "can I act on it", and for 

242 an equipment-protection interlock those come apart completely. A 

243 BLEPS record raises a MAJOR alarm PRECISELY WHEN ITS FLAG ASSERTS: 

244 that is what the alarm is for, to put the trip on an operator's 

245 screen. `EpicsCaControlPort` collapses MINOR and MAJOR to 

246 `Uncertain` (only INVALID is `Bad`, because only INVALID says the 

247 value itself is untrustworthy), so a `!= "Good"` floor here does not 

248 discard SOME readings, it discards EXACTLY THE ASSERTED ONES and 

249 keeps only the quiet ones. The observer could see a clear beamline 

250 and nothing else. 

251 

252 Measured on arcturus 2026-08-23, 67 PVs, no exceptions, and stated 

253 PER ROLE because this one function reads three kinds of PV and they 

254 do not share a label pair: 

255 

256 - process axis (`*_TRIP`, `*_WRN`, `NO_FAULT` / `TRIP`): every 

257 reading of `TRIP` carried `STATE MAJOR`; every `NO_FAULT` 

258 carried no alarm. All eight cooling-water circuits, three of 

259 seven vacuum sections, two ion pumps and six ion gauges were 

260 asserted, and a Good-only floor saw none of them. 

261 - trust axis (`*_OVER_RANGE`, `*_UNDER_RANGE`, `*_FAULTED`, 

262 `*_FAIL_TO_CLOSE`, `""` / `Present`): all sixteen read clear, 

263 all with no alarm. 

264 - system axis (`COMMUNICATIONS_FAULT`, same pair): clear, no 

265 alarm. 

266 

267 The PSS agrees on the process axis: `S02BM-PSS:Sta[AB]:SecureM` and 

268 `SR-ACIS:2BM:FesPermitM` all sit at MAJOR while asserting. Nothing 

269 at 2-BM reported MINOR. 

270 

271 What that sweep does NOT establish, and the difference matters for 

272 how far to trust the paragraph below: no trust-axis or system-axis 

273 flag was observed ASSERTED, because none were faulted that day. So 

274 "asserted implies MAJOR" is measured for the process axis and only 

275 inferred for the other two from the shared IOC template. The 

276 direction this function actually depends on is the other one, that 

277 a CLEAR reading carries no alarm, and that IS measured for all 

278 three. 

279 

280 So the floor is `Bad`, matching `_enclosure_permit_observer` 

281 (loosened for this exact reason after 2-BM's SecureM read `Unknown` 

282 forever) and `_capture_baseline_reader` (which already records that 

283 "a MAJOR alarm is still a believable value"). Both of those wrote 

284 the principle down; this observer was written later and took the 

285 strict floor without asking which question it was answering. 

286 

287 The loosening widens BOTH directions, and the second one deserves 

288 naming rather than burying. It can now call a Supply DOWN on an 

289 alarmed reading, which is the whole point. It can also now believe 

290 an alarmed CLEAR reading, which previously withheld the verdict, and 

291 that lands differently on each axis: 

292 

293 - process axis: an alarmed clear trip flag counts toward "every 

294 channel clear", so a Supply can reach `Recovering` where it 

295 previously stayed silent. 

296 - trust axis: an alarmed clear `fault_pv` no longer blinds its 

297 channel, so that channel's trip reading is believed instead of 

298 being dropped from the fold (`_verdict`). 

299 - system axis: an alarmed clear comms flag no longer reads as a 

300 dark feed, so observations flow instead of being suppressed 

301 (`_communications_lost`). 

302 

303 Each of those is a real widening, none is reachable on the measured 

304 2-BM data (every clear reading there carries no alarm, on all three 

305 axes), and all three are pinned by tests so a future facility where 

306 it IS reachable fails loudly rather than drifting. The counterweight 

307 is that the strict floor's conservatism was not free: it meant ANY 

308 standing alarm on a fault or comms record blinded the observer 

309 permanently, which is the same failure that made the process axis 

310 useless, just further upstream. 

311 

312 CORA actuates nothing on any of these verdicts: it records what the 

313 interlock reported, and the interlock, not CORA, protects the 

314 equipment. 

315 

316 ## Enum-valued records decode by index, not by label 

317 

318 BLEPS flags are binary on the PLC side, but how they surface over 

319 Channel Access depends on the record type the IOC declares. A 

320 `longin` / `ai` arrives as a number. A `bi` / `mbbi` is `DBR_ENUM`, 

321 and `EpicsCaControlPort` resolves those to their FORMAT_CTRL label 

322 STRING while carrying the index it resolved from on 

323 `Measurement.ordinal`. `cora.shared.binary_signal.binary_code` 

324 reads the index first and treats its conventional label set 

325 (`ON` / `OFF`, `TRUE` / `FALSE`, `YES` / `NO`) as the fallback. 

326 

327 BLEPS-4 IS ANSWERED, and reading by index is why this observer works 

328 at 2-BM at all. Measured on arcturus 2026-08-23 against the running 

329 `2bmBLEPS` IOC (`iocBoot/ioc2bmBLEPS/bleps.substitutions`, records 

330 built from `bleps_bi.db`): the trip and warning flags declare 

331 `ZNAM="NO_FAULT"` / `ONAM="TRIP"`, and the fault and comms flags 

332 declare `ZNAM=""` (the EMPTY STRING) / `ONAM="Present"`. Neither 

333 pair is in any conventional set, and `caget` returns those literal 

334 words, so a label-only reader resolves EVERY BLEPS channel to 

335 `None`: the comms flag included, which would then read as a dark 

336 feed and suppress the whole observer. The indices behind those 

337 labels are a plain 0 / 1, the same as any other facility's. 

338 

339 So the labels are not decoded here and no BLEPS vocabulary is 

340 hardcoded anywhere in CORA. That is deliberate beyond this one IOC: 

341 `ZNAM` / `ONAM` are free text, nothing constrains them, and a 

342 facility is free to edit them for a nicer operator screen. The index 

343 is the half that does not move. 

344 """ 

345 if not believable(reading.quality): 

346 return None 

347 code = binary_code(reading.value, ordinal=reading.ordinal) 

348 return None if code is None else code != 0

400 async def _drain(self, scope: SupplyObserverScope) -> AsyncGenerator[SupplyObservation]: 

401 channels = [c for c in self._channels if c.supply_code in scope.supply_codes] 

402 if not channels: 

403 return 

404 

405 pvs = self._subscribed_pvs(channels) 

406 all_supply_codes = frozenset(c.supply_code for c in channels) 

407 pv_supply_codes = self._pv_supply_codes(channels) 

408 # Latest believed value per PV. Absent means never read; None 

409 # means read but not believable. Both are "unknown", and neither 

410 # is "low". This is a read cache, not a verdict: it is rebuilt 

411 # from scratch on every re-subscribe and nothing is inferred from 

412 # its absence. 

413 latest: dict[str, bool | None] = {} 

414 

415 queue: asyncio.Queue[_QueueItem] = asyncio.Queue() 

416 pump_tasks = [ 

417 asyncio.create_task(self._pump(pv, queue), name=f"bleps-pump:{pv}") for pv in pvs 

418 ] 

419 poll_tasks = self._poll_tasks(channels, queue) 

420 tasks = pump_tasks + poll_tasks 

421 # Only pumps ever signal completion; a poller runs until the 

422 # `finally` below cancels it, so it must not hold this open. 

423 remaining = len(pump_tasks) 

424 try: 

425 while remaining > 0: 

426 item = await queue.get() 

427 if isinstance(item, _PumpDone): 

428 remaining -= 1 

429 continue 

430 if isinstance(item, SupplyObservation): 

431 # A poll tick, already shaped. It bypasses `latest` 

432 # and `_observations` entirely: a probe is a reach 

433 # fact, not a reading, and folding one in would let a 

434 # timer move a Supply. 

435 yield item 

436 continue 

437 pv, value = item 

438 latest[pv] = value 

439 # A reading affects only the Supply(s) its own channel 

440 # feeds, EXCEPT the comms flag, which is a fact about the 

441 # whole feed and so affects every configured Supply. 

442 # Scoping this way is what keeps the probe trail honest: 

443 # without it, any one Supply's chatty PV would refresh 

444 # every OTHER Supply's `entries_supply_probes` row too, 

445 # making a genuinely silent Supply look continuously 

446 # watched for as long as its siblings kept talking. 

447 affected = ( 

448 all_supply_codes 

449 if pv == self._communications_fault_pv 

450 else pv_supply_codes.get(pv, frozenset()) 

451 ) 

452 for observation in self._observations( 

453 channels, latest, affected_supply_codes=affected 

454 ): 

455 yield observation 

456 # Every pump has finished, but a still-running poller can have 

457 # enqueued a probe in the same instant the final `_PumpDone` 

458 # was read (`put_nowait` needs no await, so it is not ordered 

459 # against the `remaining` check above). Drain exactly what is 

460 # ALREADY queued, synchronously, into a list before yielding 

461 # any of it: yielding suspends this generator and hands 

462 # control back to a poller, which could otherwise keep 

463 # `qsize()` perpetually non-zero and stop `_drain` from ever 

464 # returning to let the runtime re-subscribe. 

465 # 

466 # Only probes can be left over, never readings: `remaining` 

467 # reaches zero only once every pump's `_PumpDone` has been 

468 # READ, and each pump queues its readings ahead of its own 

469 # sentinel, so FIFO order has already delivered them. 

470 leftover = [queue.get_nowait() for _ in range(queue.qsize())] 

471 for item in leftover: 

472 if isinstance(item, SupplyObservation): 472 ↛ 471line 472 didn't jump to line 471 because the condition on line 472 was always true

473 yield item 

474 finally: 

475 for task in tasks: 

476 task.cancel() 

477 await asyncio.gather(*tasks, return_exceptions=True)

479 def _subscribed_pvs(self, channels: Iterable[BlepsChannel]) -> list[str]: 

480 """Every PV behind `channels`: trust before process, system before channel. 

481 

482 The comms flag comes first, then each channel's instrumentation 

483 fault ahead of its trip and warning. Order does not change any 

484 verdict now that "clear" requires every channel, but it still 

485 decides how quickly the first real verdict can form. 

486 

487 Also the poller's read set, called there with one Supply's 

488 channels rather than all of them. The comms flag belongs in 

489 both: a Supply whose feed is dark cannot be assessed, so a tick 

490 that could not read it has not reached that Supply either. 

491 """ 

492 pvs: list[str] = [] 

493 if self._communications_fault_pv: 

494 pvs.append(self._communications_fault_pv) 

495 for channel in channels: 

496 if channel.fault_pv: 

497 pvs.append(channel.fault_pv) 

498 pvs.append(channel.trip_pv) 

499 if channel.warning_pv: 

500 pvs.append(channel.warning_pv) 

501 # Dedupe, preserving that order. 

502 return list(dict.fromkeys(pvs))

504 def _pv_supply_codes(self, channels: Iterable[BlepsChannel]) -> dict[str, frozenset[str]]: 

505 """Map each channel-owned PV to the Supply code(s) it feeds. 

506 

507 Excludes the comms flag deliberately: that PV's effect is 

508 system-wide and `_drain` handles it as its own case, not via 

509 this per-channel map. 

510 """ 

511 by_pv: dict[str, set[str]] = {} 

512 for channel in channels: 

513 for pv in (channel.fault_pv, channel.trip_pv, channel.warning_pv): 

514 if pv: 

515 by_pv.setdefault(pv, set()).add(channel.supply_code) 

516 return {pv: frozenset(codes) for pv, codes in by_pv.items()}

518 def _observations( 

519 self, 

520 channels: Sequence[BlepsChannel], 

521 latest: Mapping[str, bool | None], 

522 *, 

523 affected_supply_codes: frozenset[str], 

524 ) -> list[SupplyObservation]: 

525 """Recompute and report the affected Supplies' verdict, or their reach probe. 

526 

527 Stateless, and scoped to `affected_supply_codes` (the Supply or 

528 Supplies the triggering reading's own channel feeds, or every 

529 configured Supply when the reading was the comms flag): a 

530 Supply not in scope gets no entry from this call at all, so an 

531 unrelated Supply's chatty channel can never refresh this one's 

532 `entries_supply_probes` row. 

533 

534 Every affected Supply gets exactly one entry: a real verdict 

535 (`reach_tier=RELAYED`) when one can be concluded, or a probe-only 

536 entry (`observed_status=None`, `reach_tier=UNREACHED`) when it 

537 cannot. Silently emitting nothing for the inconclusive case, as 

538 an earlier version did, is exactly the coverage gap the probe 

539 trail exists to close: a Supply CORA cannot currently assess 

540 must not look identical, from the trail's perspective, to one 

541 nobody configured a channel for. 

542 """ 

543 by_supply: dict[str, list[BlepsChannel]] = {} 

544 for channel in channels: 

545 if channel.supply_code in affected_supply_codes: 

546 by_supply.setdefault(channel.supply_code, []).append(channel) 

547 if self._communications_lost(latest): 

548 return [ 

549 self._probe_only( 

550 supply_code, 

551 pv=supply_channels[0].trip_pv, 

552 reach_tier=ReachTier.UNREACHED, 

553 ) 

554 for supply_code, supply_channels in sorted(by_supply.items()) 

555 ] 

556 observations: list[SupplyObservation] = [] 

557 for supply_code, supply_channels in sorted(by_supply.items()): 

558 verdict = self._verdict(supply_channels, latest) 

559 if verdict is None: 

560 observations.append( 

561 self._probe_only( 

562 supply_code, 

563 pv=supply_channels[0].trip_pv, 

564 reach_tier=ReachTier.UNREACHED, 

565 ) 

566 ) 

567 continue 

568 trip_culprits, warning_culprits = verdict 

569 if trip_culprits:

572 supply_code,

574 reason=f"BLEPS trip: {', '.join(label for label, _ in trip_culprits)}", 

575 pv=trip_culprits[0][1], 

576 ) 

577 ) 

578 elif warning_culprits: 

579 observations.append( 

580 self._observation( 

581 supply_code, 

582 _DEGRADED, 

583 reason=( 

584 f"BLEPS warning: {', '.join(label for label, _ in warning_culprits)}" 

585 ), 

586 pv=warning_culprits[0][1], 

587 ) 

588 ) 

589 else: 

590 observations.append( 

591 self._observation( 

592 supply_code, 

593 _RECOVERING, 

594 reason="BLEPS trips clear; awaiting operator confirmation", 

595 pv=supply_channels[0].trip_pv, 

596 ) 

597 ) 

598 return observations

600 def _communications_lost(self, latest: Mapping[str, bool | None]) -> bool: 

601 """Is the whole BLEPS reading untrustworthy right now? 

602 

603 True when the comms-fault flag is asserted, and also when it is 

604 configured but not yet believably read: a comms flag we cannot 

605 read is precisely the condition it exists to report. 

606 

607 Warns on the way into that state so a dark feed is visible. 

608 Without it, a typo'd comms PV and a healthy beamline are 

609 indistinguishable, both being total silence. 

610 """ 

611 if not self._communications_fault_pv: 

612 return False 

613 lost = latest.get(self._communications_fault_pv) is not False 

614 if lost and not self._reported_dark: 

615 self._reported_dark = True 

616 _log.warning( 

617 "bleps_observer.feed_dark", 

618 communications_fault_pv=self._communications_fault_pv, 

619 detail="BLEPS observations suppressed until the comms flag reads clear", 

620 ) 

621 elif not lost and self._reported_dark: 621 ↛ 622line 621 didn't jump to line 622 because the condition on line 621 was never true

622 self._reported_dark = False 

623 _log.info("bleps_observer.feed_restored") 

624 return lost

626 def _verdict( 

627 self, channels: Sequence[BlepsChannel], latest: Mapping[str, bool | None] 

628 ) -> tuple[tuple[tuple[str, str], ...], tuple[tuple[str, str], ...]] | None: 

629 """Fold channels into `(trip_culprits, warning_culprits)`, or `None` when blind. 

630 

631 Each element is a tuple of `(label, pv)` for every believable 

632 channel asserting that level. Both empty means every channel is 

633 believable and clear. `None` means the verdict is inconclusive. 

634 

635 Trip dominates unconditionally: a believable trip anywhere is 

636 reported regardless of a sibling channel's blindness or warning 

637 state, exactly the pre-warning asymmetry (one believable trip is 

638 enough to call the Supply down; a single unbelievable channel is 

639 enough to withhold "clear"). Warning extends the SAME asymmetry 

640 one rung down: absent any trip, one believable warning anywhere 

641 is enough to call the Supply Degraded, and "clear" (both empty) 

642 still requires every channel's trip AND configured warning 

643 reading to be believable. A tripped channel's own warning 

644 reading is skipped: the same physical quantity cannot be both, 

645 and the trip already dominates. 

646 """ 

647 trip_culprits: list[tuple[str, str]] = [] 

648 warning_culprits: list[tuple[str, str]] = [] 

649 blind = False 

650 for channel in channels: 

651 if channel.fault_pv and latest.get(channel.fault_pv) is not False: 

652 blind = True # instrument faulted, or its fault flag unread 

653 continue 

654 trip = latest.get(channel.trip_pv) 

655 if trip is None: 

656 blind = True # unread, or read but not believable 

657 continue 

658 if trip: 

659 trip_culprits.append((channel.label, channel.trip_pv)) 

660 continue 

661 if channel.warning_pv is None: 

662 continue 

663 warning = latest.get(channel.warning_pv) 

664 if warning is None: 

665 blind = True # unread, or read but not believable 

666 continue 

667 if warning: 

668 warning_culprits.append((channel.label, channel.warning_pv)) 

669 if trip_culprits: 

670 return tuple(trip_culprits), () 

671 if blind: 

672 return None 

673 return (), tuple(warning_culprits)

688 def _probe_only(self, supply_code: str, *, pv: str, reach_tier: ReachTier) -> SupplyObservation: 

689 """A reach fact with no status claim. 

690 

691 Two callers, two different facts, one tier vocabulary. 

692 `_observations` builds these when a verdict is inconclusive and 

693 always grades them `UNREACHED`; `_poll` builds them on a timer 

694 and grades the tick itself. `ReachTier` measures reach, not its 

695 cause, so the two meanings share `UNREACHED` legitimately: the 

696 enclosure precedent overloads it the same way, for a failed poll 

697 and for a disconnect. 

698 

699 `reason` is empty because nothing reads it: the runtime returns 

700 before building a transition command when `observed_status` is 

701 `None`. `pv` names the channel this probe row is attributed to; 

702 callers pass the Supply's first trip PV, mirroring how a real 

703 `Unavailable`/`Recovering` verdict already attributes to one 

704 representative channel rather than the whole set. `_poll` 

705 departs from that on failure alone, naming the PV that actually 

706 went unread, because an `UNREACHED` row is only actionable if it 

707 says which channel to go and look at. 

708 """ 

709 return SupplyObservation( 

710 supply_code=supply_code, 

711 observed_status=None, 

712 reach_tier=reach_tier, 

713 observed_at=self._clock.now(), 

714 reason="", 

715 source_kind=_SOURCE_KIND, 

716 source_id=pv, 

717 )

740 async def _poll( 

741 self, 

742 supply_code: str, 

743 channels: Sequence[BlepsChannel], 

744 queue: asyncio.Queue[_QueueItem], 

745 ) -> None: 

746 """Re-affirm reach to one Supply's whole channel set every tick. 

747 

748 Never pushes `_PumpDone`: this task is a sibling of the pumps, 

749 not a stage in any pump's lifecycle, and runs until `_drain`'s 

750 `finally` cancels it on teardown. It ticks unconditionally, 

751 regardless of how much push traffic the channels are producing, 

752 which is simpler than gating on quiescence and avoids the "a 

753 chatty Supply is never polled" surprise that gating would carry. 

754 

755 Reads are concurrent so the tick costs one round trip rather 

756 than thirty-two, and `return_exceptions=True` makes every 

757 per-PV failure a datum instead of an escape: a probe recording 

758 that CORA could not see a channel is the entire point, so there 

759 is nothing here to raise about. Hence no `except` clause, unlike 

760 `_pump`. Teardown still works: a cancellation aimed at THIS task 

761 propagates out of `gather` rather than being collected. A 

762 cancellation raised inside one read is collected like any other 

763 failure, which is the right reading of it, that channel went 

764 unread this tick. 

765 

766 One row per tick, `RELAYED` only if every PV answered. A partial 

767 read is graded `UNREACHED` and attributed to the first PV that 

768 failed: reporting reach because some channels answered would put 

769 exactly the false coverage claim in the trail that the trail 

770 exists to rule out. 

771 """ 

772 assert self._probe_tick_seconds is not None 

773 pvs = self._subscribed_pvs(channels) 

774 representative_pv = channels[0].trip_pv 

775 while True: 

776 await asyncio.sleep(self._probe_tick_seconds) 

777 results = await asyncio.gather( 

778 *(self._control_port.read(pv) for pv in pvs), return_exceptions=True 

779 ) 

780 unread = [ 

781 pv 

782 for pv, result in zip(pvs, results, strict=True) 

783 if isinstance(result, BaseException) 

784 ] 

785 if unread: 

786 _log.warning( 

787 "bleps_observer.probe_unreached", 

788 supply_code=supply_code, 

789 unread_pvs=unread, 

790 read_count=len(pvs), 

791 detail="probe tick could not read every channel behind this supply", 

792 ) 

793 queue.put_nowait( 

794 self._probe_only( 

795 supply_code, 

796 pv=unread[0] if unread else representative_pv, 

797 reach_tier=ReachTier.UNREACHED if unread else ReachTier.RELAYED, 

798 ) 

799 )

801 async def _pump(self, pv: str, queue: asyncio.Queue[_QueueItem]) -> None: 

802 """Forward one PV's believability to the merge queue. 

803 

804 A clean stream end keeps the last reading rather than voiding it, 

805 which is where this differs from `_enclosure_permit_observer`. A 

806 clean end means no further updates, not that the last value was 

807 wrong, and voiding it here would be actively harmful: a Supply's 

808 verdict needs several PVs believable at once, so discarding a 

809 good reading the moment its stream closed would leave the 

810 aggregate unable to conclude anything. 

811 

812 ANY failure voids the reading, not just a disconnect. The port has 

813 no single base error class, only a closed tuple private to the 

814 Conductor, and duplicating that tuple here is precisely the drift 

815 its own fitness test exists to catch. Nothing is lost by being 

816 broad: an unroutable address, a coercion failure and an adapter 

817 bug are all equally reasons to stop believing a channel, so the 

818 distinction would only change the log level. What matters is that 

819 the failure is recorded and the channel voided rather than 

820 escaping into `asyncio.gather(return_exceptions=True)`, where it 

821 would be discarded unseen and leave a stale reading standing. 

822 """ 

823 try:

825 state = flag_state_from_reading(reading)

828 # naming "Good" independently: an alarmed reading is 

829 # believable here, so an alarmed one CORA still cannot 

830 # resolve is exactly as loud a problem as a quiet one. 

831 # Testing "Good" would have gone quiet for every 

832 # asserted BLEPS flag, which is the population most 

833 # worth hearing about. 

834 # 

835 # A believable reading CORA still cannot resolve. Two 

836 # shapes reach here, and the ordinal tells them apart: 

837 # with no ordinal the reading carried only a label 

838 # outside the conventional set, which now means a 

839 # genuine string record rather than the enum case 

840 # BLEPS-4 covered; WITH an ordinal it is out of the 

841 # two-state range, which says this channel is pointed 

842 # at a record that is not a flag at all (an `mbbi` 

843 # mid-vocabulary), a configuration error rather than a 

844 # vocabulary one. Both are logged loudly because the 

845 # alternative is a monitor that looks healthy and 

846 # reports nothing. 

847 _log.warning( 

848 "bleps_observer.uninterpretable_flag", 

849 pv=pv,

851 kind=reading.kind,

853 detail=( 

854 "not resolvable to a two-state flag: out-of-range ordinal, " 

855 "or no ordinal and an unconventional label; excluded"

857 ) 

858 queue.put_nowait((pv, state)) 

859 except asyncio.CancelledError: 

860 raise 

861 except Exception: 

862 _log.exception("bleps_observer.channel_lost", pv=pv) 

863 queue.put_nowait((pv, None)) 

864 finally: 

865 queue.put_nowait(_PUMP_DONE)
