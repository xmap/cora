1"""SupplyObserver port: substrate-driven Supply-status observation stream. 

2 

3`SupplyObserver` is the BC-local async Protocol the Supply BC's 

4monitor-trigger runtime uses to drain status observations from the 

5substrate (EPICS PV monitors, PVA subscriptions, Tango attribute 

6callbacks). Substrate details live behind concrete adapters; the 

7runtime never touches substrate-specific symbols. 

8 

9Shaped after `cora.enclosure.ports.enclosure_observer`, which is the 

10in-codebase precedent for a monitor-trigger seam, and BC-local for the 

11same three reasons: the sole consumer is this BC's own runtime, the 

12seam is substrate-IO rather than cross-BC contract, and no other BC 

13reads observations off it. 

14 

15## Only a status, never a severity 

16 

17The observation carries a `SupplyStatus` string and nothing else about 

18how bad things are. Substrate severity vocabularies are flattened by 

19the adapter before crossing this seam: no `severity`, no `alarm_class`, 

20no `vendor_status_code`. Severity bookkeeping belongs to the substrate. 

21 

22This matters more than it looks for the first real consumer. BLEPS 

23sorts its channels into warnings, trips and faults, but those are not 

24one scale (see [[project_bleps_ingest_design]]): warning and trip 

25answer "how bad is the measured value" while fault answers "is the 

26reading believable at all". Only the first question maps onto a 

27`SupplyStatus`. An adapter that decides a reading is not believable 

28must NOT emit an observation at all; there is no status value meaning 

29"I cannot tell", and inventing one would flatten a trust problem onto 

30the availability scale. Withholding is how this port says "unknown". 

31 

32## What a monitor may and may not drive 

33 

34The decider fences `Available`, `Unknown` and `Decommissioned` out of 

35the monitor path, so an adapter can only ever drive a Supply toward 

36`Degraded`, `Unavailable` or `Recovering`. Coming all the way back to 

37`Available` is an operator's word (`restore_supply`), on the latched- 

38alarm precedent in [[project_supply_design]]. An adapter that observes 

39a resource looking healthy again therefore emits `Recovering`, not 

40`Available`, and a person confirms. 

41 

42## Stub roster 

43 

44`AlwaysQuietSupplyObserver` ships inline at the bottom of this module: 

45the zero-substrate stub for tests, standing in for a beamline where 

46nothing is wrong. It yields NOTHING, and the name says so, following 

47`AlwaysQuietCautionLookup` rather than the `All<Predicate>Lookup` 

48family: those quantify over a set, this one describes a stream's 

49posture. An earlier name said `AllAvailable`, which was worse than 

50imprecise, because `Available` is the one status a monitor may never 

51drive, so the name promised the forbidden thing and then spent a 

52paragraph unsaying it. 

53 

54## Subscribe shape 

55 

56`observe` is a plain `def` returning `AsyncIterator[SupplyObservation]` 

57directly (no surrounding coroutine), matching the Enclosure port. 

58Connect setup may happen lazily on the first `__anext__`. Mid-stream 

59disconnect is the adapter's concern: production adapters re-raise so 

60the runtime can reconnect. 

61"""

71@dataclass(frozen=True) 

72class SupplyObservation: 

73 """One status reading, or reach probe, drained from the substrate. 

74 

75 `supply_code` is the BC-local Supply identity surface adapters 

76 know. The Supply address is a four-tuple 

77 (`facility_code`, `containing_asset_id`, `kind`, `name`), which is 

78 more than an adapter should have to assemble, so the runtime is 

79 handed a `{supply_code: supply_id}` map and adapters name the 

80 resource by its code alone. At 2-BM the codes are the Supply names 

81 (`2-BM cooling water`, `2-BM beamline vacuum`). 

82 

83 `observed_status` is the raw status string the adapter produced 

84 after flattening whatever the substrate said. The runtime parses it 

85 against the `SupplyStatus` codomain and skips the observation if it 

86 does not match, so an adapter must not invent values. `None` means 

87 this observation is probe-only and makes no status claim at all: 

88 the aggregating verdict this tick was inconclusive (some channel 

89 behind this Supply could not be believed), which is a fact worth 

90 recording in the probe trail even though nothing can be said about 

91 the Supply's actual status. A `None` status never causes a Supply 

92 transition, by construction: there is nothing to parse. 

93 

94 `reach_tier` is required, not optional, mirroring the Enclosure 

95 seam: it grades CORA's own reach to the substrate this tick, 

96 independent of what (if anything) was believed. `RELAYED` on every 

97 real verdict (tripped or clear -- a channel spoke and was believed); 

98 `UNREACHED` on the inconclusive case (a channel behind this Supply 

99 could not be believed, or the whole feed is dark). 

100 

101 `observed_at` is the adapter's wall-clock at observation time, 

102 carried for diagnostics and for adapters that batch. The runtime 

103 does NOT use it as the event's `occurred_at`: a substrate-supplied 

104 timestamp could backdate an event past ones already appended, so 

105 ordering stays on the trusted clock. See the watch item in 

106 [[project_bleps_ingest_design]]. 

107 

108 `reason` is the adapter's human-readable account of what it saw 

109 ("Flow2 below set point"). It lands verbatim in the transition's 

110 reason when `observed_status` is set, which is where the per-channel 

111 detail lives: the status says a run cannot draw on the resource, the 

112 reason says which of the eight circuits said so. Unused (and may be 

113 empty) on a probe-only observation, since no transition reads it. 

114 

115 `source_kind` and `source_id` ship as separate strings and are 

116 joined into the colon-delimited `monitor_ref` payload string by the 

117 runtime, matching the Enclosure seam. 

118 """

141class SupplyObserver(Protocol): 

142 """Async source of `SupplyObservation` values from the substrate. 

143 

144 Not `@runtime_checkable`, unlike `EnclosureObserver`: nothing does 

145 an `isinstance` conformance check against this port, and the port 

146 fitness test requires the decorator and such a check to travel 

147 together so dead decorators do not accumulate. Add it if and when a 

148 runtime check appears. 

149 

150 The substrate adapter owns the subscription lifecycle. The runtime 

151 iterates and records each observation as a Supply transition. 

152 

153 Iteration semantics (identical to `EnclosureObserver`): 

154 

155 - The iterator is open-ended for live substrates; callers either 

156 `async for` it for the runtime's lifetime or cancel the task to 

157 tear down the subscription. 

158 - One-shot observers (tests, stubs) MAY exhaust the iterator 

159 after a finite number of observations; the runtime treats 

160 `StopAsyncIteration` as a clean teardown. 

161 - Disconnect handling is the adapter's concern; production 

162 adapters re-raise substrate disconnect errors through the 

163 iterator so the runtime can reconnect. 

164 """

171class AlwaysQuietSupplyObserver: 

172 """Stub `SupplyObserver` that observes nothing. 

173 

174 The zero-substrate observer for tests, standing in for a beamline 

175 where every resource is fine. A beamline with nothing wrong produces 

176 no readings, so silence is the faithful representation, and it is 

177 also the only safe one: `Available` is fenced out of the monitor path 

178 (only an operator restores a Supply), so a stub that yielded it would 

179 raise on every tick.
