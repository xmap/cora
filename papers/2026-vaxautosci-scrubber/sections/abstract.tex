Autonomous and closed-loop scientific instruments, from self-driving labs to
agent-supervised synchrotron runs, generate provenance at machine speed: dense
in decisions, multi-pass, and shared between people and agents, yet largely
invisible to the humans who must later audit it. A scientist returning to an
unattended overnight run asks plain questions: did the alignment converge, what
was in flight when the beam dropped, who decided to resume, can I prove what was
recorded? Conventional post-hoc provenance graphs flatten the temporal and
causal texture those questions depend on. We observe that event-sourced systems
of record already answer such questions internally, because they reconstruct any
past state by folding an immutable, ordered event stream. We turn that fold into
an interaction. This paper contributes (1) a task abstraction for auditing
closed-loop runs, and (2) a \emph{replay scrubber}: a draggable time cursor that
replays a run to any recorded instant, showing a run-lifecycle lane of who or
what drove each transition, nested convergence brackets colored by verdict,
open-interval markers for unfinished steps, and a content-addressed fidelity
badge that lets the view verify the replayed state matches the record. We ground
the prototype in a real lights-out, agent-supervised run on the APS 2-BM micro-CT
beamline, in which CORA conducts a rotation-axis centering alignment, a
RunSupervisor agent holds the run on beam loss and auto-resumes it, and the
scrubber localizes the interrupted projection by folding to the beam-loss
instant. We discuss how event-sourced records reshape provenance visualization
for autonomous science.
